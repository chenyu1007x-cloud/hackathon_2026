Background and inputs

We start with synthetic skill profiles

We study skill concentration and gaps that can arise from manual, random, or single-skill team formation. At a hackathon, one team might include several developers while another lacks programming skills. We explore an inspectable rule using multiple skills, while leaving participants in control.

We represent each person with several skill dimensions. We show the first rows of the source CSV alongside the full skill table for the selected case.

We use synthetic profiles, not records of real people. These inputs let us test algorithms and rules without establishing real collaboration outcomes or fairness.

02

Objective and constraint

We define what to optimize

We reward skill differences within each team and penalize imbalance between the teams in each skill total. We assign everyone once and require equal team sizes.

E(x) = −C(x) + B(x)
+ P(Σᵢxᵢ − n/2)²
C · Within-team difference
We sum the mean absolute skill difference for every pair assigned to the same team.
B · Skill imbalance
We sum the squared deviations of each team’s skill totals from half the combined total.
P · Team-size penalty

We minimize E. Our within-team reward differs from the usual Max-Cut reward for separating pairs. Skill difference is only a proxy for complementarity.

06

Classical comparison and verification

We compare against classical references

We inspect the objective, team-size feasibility, absolute energy gap to the exact reference, and measured runtime. We retain infeasible returns, but their energy cannot be treated as a better valid partition.

We report the absolute energy gap ΔE = E(returned) − E* for feasible returns, not a relative percentage. A missing exact reference or infeasible return has no applicable gap. Different budgets measure different work; local timings do not establish quantum advantage.

We keep our conclusions bounded

We verify the mathematical rules and computation for these synthetic cases. Real team formation also needs preferences, availability, accessibility, and project requirements. Equal team size and balanced skill totals cannot replace participants’ judgment.
